## Scope and language coherence

### Findings

No material scope finding was found.

The review attempted to split execution ownership from parking. The split does not hold: parking
without an affine owner cannot return dormant continuation ownership to ordinary source, while an
owned execution without atomic registration/relinquishment cannot wait on source-defined Deferred,
timer, or readiness state without a lost-wake interval. They are two powers of one independently
resumable execution thesis.

The proposal remains subtractive around that thesis. Canonical Fiber policy, provider inheritance,
structured lifetimes, parallel transfer, timer APIs, and asynchronous host entry are explicitly
dependent decisions. The future Coroutine example serves only as alternate-owner pressure and does
not add yield typing or a public coroutine API.

The owned detachable environment restriction is internally coherent. `Intrinsic.execution` is the
explicit sealed erasure boundary for one exact closed Effect representation; lexical value and
provider loans are rejected. The Fiber witness does not undermine the rule by capturing its
borrowed Scheduler or Allocator. This is narrower than a final Fiber API, but that limitation is
stated rather than hidden.

## Examples and programmer model

### Findings

The examples independently establish the intended distinctions:

- current `Effect.suspend` supplies nested child transfer but no independently owned dormant state;
- `Intrinsic.execution` supplies affine owner-selected drive and exact destruction;
- `Intrinsic.park` supplies register-before-relinquish and external readiness;
- `Deferred` retains payload and waiter policy entirely in source;
- joinable fork pays for typed result state, while resultless infallible spawn does not;
- direct source allocations expose Allocator and allocation-failure rows, while wake and join do not
  allocate; and
- direct, nested-only, and external-wake-reachable programs retain distinct lowering costs.

The adversarial event sequences are closed. Early wake is latched until the complete `onSuspend`
callback returns; destruction inside that callback cancels notification; late wake after destruction
is a safe no-op; eligible destruction may leave only a stale source identity; and `driveOne`
discards that identity. Never-driven execution cleanup has a named authority.

The callback types also support the claimed no-inline-drive rule. `onReady` is an ordinary
non-Effect callback: it can enqueue source state but cannot invoke the effectful `Intrinsic.drive`
operation. The only execution handle has already moved through `onSuspend`, so ordinary source
cannot create a second activation.

### Missing or corrected cases

One optional editorial correction remains. The boundary example that calls `program()` without
providers says only that Scheduler is unsatisfied, but Revision 20's `program` also requires
`&mut Allocator` and exposes `OutOfMemoryError`. The example is still correctly rejected and the
omission changes no semantic decision.

Automatic provider inheritance and nested Fiber operations remain intentionally absent. They are
mandatory pressure cases for the dependent concurrency-library SLP, not missing evidence for this
compiler-substrate decision.

## Compiler privilege and realizability

### Findings

No material privilege or feasibility blocker was found.

The three operations remain minimal under attack:

- Removing `Execution` loses ordinary-source ownership, selection, and exact dormant destruction.
- Removing callback-shaped `drive` requires another step-result or continuation-transfer surface.
- Separating wake registration from `park` reopens the lost-notification race.
- Removing the stable wake-control cell makes an escaped readiness callback unsafe after execution
  destruction.

The hidden cell does not smuggle Scheduler policy into the compiler. It records only registration,
eligibility, cancellation, and reference lifetime. Payloads, identities, queues, unlink guards,
fairness, and resumption choice remain source-owned. A later parallel implementation must make the
same transitions atomic, but local-only programs need no atomic representation.

The execution erasure boundary is explicit rather than the implicit representation joining rejected
by [generics, interfaces, and specialization](../../../docs/language/generics-interfaces-and-specialization.md). Target-private storage may retain concrete layout, resume, and cleanup metadata behind
the opaque affine handle. Exact capture facts decide detachability before erasure, and every accepted
capture has one cleanup owner.

The proposal also preserves allocation transparency. Shared Scheduler, Deferred, result, task, and
ready-link state is allocated through ordinary source APIs. Compiler/runtime continuation and wake
control storage follows the existing fatal suspension-storage category rather than acquiring a
source Allocator service. The distinction is observable and consistently stated.

### Smaller boundary, if any

None found. A public Wake, execution-step sum, explicit destroy, universal coroutine, or
compiler-known Scheduler adds surface or policy without enabling a case missing from the selected
boundary.

## Synthesis

### Proposal-level blockers

None.

### Editorial improvements

- If the Candidate is edited again, make the missing-provider boundary example mention both
  Scheduler and Allocator and the unhandled allocation failure.

### OpenSpec realization questions

- Specify constructed, running, registering, dormant, eligible, cancelled, completed, and destroyed
  execution/wake transitions and their illegal edges.
- Prove detachability from exact capture and provider facts before explicit representation erasure.
- Specify cleanup tables for never-driven, completed, dormant-dropped, eligible-dropped, and
  cancelled-with-retained-wake paths.
- Preserve non-Effect callback restrictions that prevent inline drive and suspension while shared
  source state is accessed.
- Prove direct, nested-only, and external-wake pay-for-use behavior across evaluator, native, and
  Wasm without recognizing library actors by spelling.
- Keep ordinary source allocation and compiler/runtime continuation storage in their selected
  failure and requirement channels.

### Reviewer disagreements

The only recurring pressure is whether a closed-child witness is broad enough to say fibers are
enabled. It is broad enough for this SLP: fork, wait, wake, join, result publication, and destruction
are implemented without compiler-known concurrency actors. It would not be broad enough to accept a
canonical Fiber API, whose provider inheritance and scope rules remain deliberately undecided.
