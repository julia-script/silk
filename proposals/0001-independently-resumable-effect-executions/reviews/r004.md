## Scope and language coherence

### Findings

The execution/parking thesis remains cohesive: affine ownership supplies dormant lifecycle authority,
while atomic registration and relinquishment prevent lost readiness. Canonical scheduling, structured
lifetimes, parallel transfer, and host ABI remain valid dependent proposals.

Four scope contracts required revision:

1. `Execution<A>` is a narrow existential executable erasure, not merely another exact Effect
   representation. Revision 20 relied on hidden invoke and cleanup metadata without explicitly
   establishing that sealed exception to [generics, interfaces, and specialization](../../../docs/language/generics-interfaces-and-specialization.md) or its unavoidable wrapper cost.
2. A retained `onReady` callback could capture a lexical loan even though the Effect body was checked
   for detachability. Its environment must be closed and loan-free before `drive` may retain it.
3. Root parking remained conditional on a compatible root owner. That is a valid boundary only if
   unsupported entry configurations are explicitly rejected and the proposal does not claim an
   asynchronous outcome ABI.
4. Fatal execution-stack exhaustion closes an option [Effect suspension](../../../docs/language/effect-suspension.md) deliberately left open. The choice can
   remain here, but must be defended as a semantic decision rather than described as inevitable.

### Proposed split, if any

No split of execution ownership from parking is recommended. Host integration remains a dependent
proposal. Execution-storage failure policy remains in this SLP after the revision explicitly selects
and pressures it; no separate storage proposal is required.

## Examples and programmer model

### Findings

Six concrete cases exposed incomplete or inconsistent behavior.

#### E1 — Dormant drive had no defined result

`onSuspend` returns the same `Execution<A>` type before readiness, so affinity does not prevent a
well-typed owner from immediately passing that dormant handle to `drive`. Revision 20 said only
eligible executions may be driven but selected neither a type-state token nor a dynamic result.

Required revision: define the illegal transition. The smallest surface-preserving rule is a fatal
intrinsic-state trap before any callback or continuation action. The source witness must show how its
ready queue prevents ordinary library code from selecting a merely dormant task.

#### E2 — Retained callback loans and cleanup were incomplete

An `onReady` closure can capture `&mut local`, outlive `drive`, and become hidden behind the execution
handle. The witness also appeared to borrow its local ready-inbox clone. Since all three drive
callbacks may own affine captures, completion, parking, readiness, and destruction need an exact
callback cleanup matrix.

#### E3 — The reusable ready link could become dangling

Revision 20 both released a task node on record removal and allowed a queued stale identity to remain.
If queue linkage lives in the node, dequeue traverses freed storage; if the queue copies IDs into
growable storage, wake is not proven allocation-free.

Required revision: reserve one ReadyInbox slot per task at task construction. ReadyInbox owns the
slot separately from TaskStore. An eligible cancellation leaves a queued tombstone until dequeue;
an idle slot can be released with the task. Wake then uses pre-existing storage.

#### E4 — Timer requirements contradicted the closed-child rule

The timer example described a system-event capability but declared no Timer requirement. Its child
was passed directly to `Fiber.fork`, even though the witness permits only closed child Effects.

Required revision: show `? &Timer`, clone an owned Timer value, provide it to the child before fork,
and retain the Timer requirement at a root call.

#### E5 — The future Coroutine example hid shared payload allocation

The wrapper and the running execution must exchange yielded payloads, but the selected compiler
protocol transports no typed yield. Ordinary source therefore needs a shared port or equivalent
channel whose construction exposes SLP-0002 allocation and failure.

#### E6 — The requirement boundary and failure cleanup were under-specified

The missing-Scheduler example also omitted Allocator and its failure, making the stated diagnostic
non-isolating. Fork needed cleanup behavior when result construction or task reservation fails.
Resultless spawn also needed to say that enqueueing does not itself start the child.

### Missing or corrected cases

- Add the dormant-drive counterexample and selected trap.
- Make `onReady` own its inbox capture and use two distinct TaskStore clones for the mutually
  exclusive completion and suspension closures.
- Give Fiber and intrinsic examples explicit callable/Effect representation binders.
- Show queued-tombstone lifetime, allocation failure cleanup, and never-driven spawn destruction.
- Distinguish explicit execution-wrapper cost from continuation and Wake costs.

## Compiler privilege and realizability

### Findings

#### C1 — Callback-shaped Wake is not storable in ordinary Silk

The decisive finding is structural. Revision 20 stored readiness as
`Option<once fn() -> ()>`. [generics, interfaces, and specialization](../../../docs/language/generics-interfaces-and-specialization.md) requires callable fields to carry their exact representation;
the compiler rejects a bare callable placed in a nominal field or through `Option` with `SEM0103`.
Deferred exists before `park` produces the callback, so its nominal type cannot later acquire that
unknown representation parameter. A homogeneous waiter store therefore cannot implement the
claimed witness.

Required revision: add a fixed-layout opaque affine `Intrinsic.Wake` and consuming
`Intrinsic.wake`. The value refers to the already selected stable cancelled wake-control cell. This
is smaller than adding general existential callable values, callback boxing, or indirect-dispatch
semantics to ordinary Silk.

#### C2 — The execution and retained-callback erasure boundaries were implicit

Heterogeneous `Execution<()>` storage needs dynamic hidden invoke/drop metadata. Retaining a concrete
`onReady` environment needs the same kind of sealed metadata. These are explicit compiler-owned
erasures, not ordinary representation joins, and must be named as narrow exceptions that do not
weaken [generics, interfaces, and specialization](../../../docs/language/generics-interfaces-and-specialization.md) for source aggregates.

#### C3 — The pay-for-use lattice omitted ownership

An explicit `Execution` containing a non-suspending body still owns an unstarted, droppable erased
body. It cannot promise the same minimum representation as direct `run`. Representation selection
needs independent ownership and suspension-capability axes.

#### C4 — Independent execution lacked evaluator-depth semantics

[Effect suspension](../../../docs/language/effect-suspension.md) counts suspended logical invocations toward `CallDepth`. If an execution's depth instead
depends on the source stack of whichever owner currently calls `drive`, scheduling changes whether
it reaches the limit and changes trace ancestry.

Required revision: each Execution owns a logical stack rooted at first drive and restores it on
later drives; owner frames and compiler drive helpers are not child logical ancestors.

### Smaller boundary, if any

The original callback Wake was smaller only syntactically and failed the accepted representation
model. Opaque affine Wake plus consuming wake is the smallest demonstrated fixed-layout capability.
An execution-step sum, explicit destroy, Scheduler token, general callable erasure, or public
continuation remains unnecessary.

## Synthesis

### Proposal-level blockers

1. **Storable readiness:** ordinary Silk cannot retain the callback-shaped Wake selected by revision
   20.
2. **Retained callback ownership:** `onReady` can erase a lexical loan and lacked exact cleanup.
3. **Dormant-drive semantics:** affinity does not encode eligibility.
4. **Ready-slot lifetime:** stale queue entries and node release were incompatible.
5. **Representation and evaluator contracts:** explicit execution cost, sealed erasure, and logical
   depth were incomplete.
6. **Pressure-example truthfulness:** Timer closure and Coroutine shared allocation were hidden.

### Editorial improvements

- Fix the retained-values call to provide both Token and Logger.
- Replace unknown runtime-dispatch wording with conservative pre-specialization summaries.
- Mark Intrinsic catalog additions as affecting names.
- State the dependency ordering while SLP-0002 remains Candidate.
- Clarify root-target rejection, fork failure cleanup, and resultless enqueue behavior.

### OpenSpec realization questions

- Define Initial, Running, Dormant, Eligible, Completed, Destroyed, and illegal-drive transitions.
- Define the fixed Wake layout, one-shot consumption, cancellation, and late no-op behavior.
- Prove the two sealed erasure boundaries preserve exact invoke/drop metadata and reject retained
  loans.
- Prove the ownership/suspension cost matrix across evaluator, native, and Wasm.
- Implement ReadyInbox reservations and tombstones without wake-time allocation.
- Preserve execution-local evaluator depth and trace ancestry across owner switches.

### Reviewer disagreements

The scope lens questioned whether fatal storage policy and root ownership should split. The other
lenses found no need for a split if the Candidate directly selects fatal execution-stack exhaustion
and explicitly rejects unsupported root entry. The synthesis keeps both boundaries in this SLP and
strengthens their evidence. All three lenses independently found either the retained-callback loan
or the callback-representation problem; those objections require surface revision rather than prose
defense.

## Revision response

Resolved in Candidate revision 21:

1. Replaced callback-shaped readiness with opaque affine `Intrinsic.Wake` and consuming
   `Intrinsic.wake`; Deferred and ReadyInbox now store Wake values, not erased callables.
2. Required `onReady` to be closed and loan-free, identified `drive` as a narrow sealed erasure
   boundary, and specified callback cleanup for completion, parking, readiness, destruction, and
   fatal traps.
3. Defined driving Dormant as a fatal intrinsic-state trap and made the Scheduler select executions
   only from dequeued ready identities.
4. Replaced task-owned queue linkage with ReadyInbox-owned per-task reservations and queued
   tombstones whose storage survives until dequeue.
5. Added explicit ownership/suspension cost axes, narrow existential execution erasure, and one
   logical evaluator stack per Execution rooted at first drive.
6. Corrected Timer requirements and owned closure, Coroutine shared-port allocation, provider
   boundary rows, fork failure cleanup, representation binders, retained-value construction, and
   resultless-spawn behavior.
7. Retained fatal compiler/runtime execution-stack exhaustion as a deliberate decision distinct
   from recoverable source task/result/queue allocation, and narrowed root evidence to compatible
   owners plus explicit target rejection.
